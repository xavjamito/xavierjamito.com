I used to write. A lot. My old Samsung laptop - a high school graduation gift from my parents - was full of scattered Notepad files: missing chapters of an incoherent book, messy story points that would have required Marie Kondo-level intervention, and sports editorials written for an audience of zero, quietly waiting to be sent to Grantland or Bleacher Report someday, to launch what I was certain would be a budding sportswriting career.

I studied a semester of Literature in college with the full, earnest intention of becoming a writer. That was the plan. The dream. The whole thing.

I still write, by the way. Just not prose. I write code now - which, if you squint, is sort of the same thing, except the compiler has no patience for metaphor.

Life's funny that way.

To be clear, I'm not complaining. Software engineering is genuinely interesting work and it pays considerably better than a literary career - a sentence that would have horrified my 17-year-old self, who was busy underlining Steinbeck and convincing himself that poverty and struggle had a certain romance to it. They do not. I have since learned this.

But somewhere between learning what a _merge conflict_ is and writing my first pull request, the other kind of writing quietly stopped. No dramatic break, no journal entry about it. It just... didn't happen anymore. The way you stop going to the gym, not because you decided to quit, but because you were busy on Monday, and then Monday became all the other days.

What I did not stop doing, however, was having opinions. Strong ones. Frequently. About sports (the NBos Celtics in particular), about movies, about the rapidly declining state of Philippine politics, about whether a certain design pattern is a cry for help. I have been yapping - to my wife, to friends, in group chats, into the void of social media - with the same energy the whole time. Turns out I never lost the instinct to write. I just forgot to aim it somewhere it could actually land.

> _"It starts with this: put your desk in the corner, and every time you sit down there to write, remind yourself why it isn't in the middle of the room. Life isn't a support system for art. It's the other way around." - Stephen King, On Writing_

I re-read [_On Writing_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/10569.On_Writing) recently. The King (yes, in this blog, it's Stephen, not LeBron) is relentlessly practical about the craft in a way that cuts through every excuse you've been rehearsing. At some point I realized that the yapping needed a better home than a group chat that nobody scrolls back through. Hence, this blog.

So this is me getting back on the horse. The horse, in this case, being a blank page. Which historically has thrown me several times. But we're trying again.

---

What can you expect here? Roughly everything I find interesting, which is to say: **sports** (mostly basketball and football), **comics and cinema**, **pop culture** at large, and maybe even a little bit of **programming** - the writing-code kind, not the writing-writing kind, though those will sometimes collide in ways I find entertaining and you may find tedious.

I've written before, in various corners of the internet. There are [sports articles](https://medium.com/@AssortedgeMedia/late-game-heroics-from-ott-keep-azkals-atop-group-f-662f8279ecfb), [editorials](https://medium.com/@xavjamito/kobe-bryant-and-the-quest-for-closure-2ecc631ba660), and some [published research papers](https://lpulaguna.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/7.-Food-Finder-A-Mobile-App.pdf) that I produced before I decided writing had no business being in the same room as bills, Jira tickets, and deadlines. 

Before I fully convinced myself that the party was over.

This blog is not a comeback tour. It's just structured yapping. Regularly. Because apparently that's the only way to do it. King says it plainly. He says it more than once. He's not interested in your excuses.

There is no shortcut.

See you on the other side.
